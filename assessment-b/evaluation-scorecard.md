# Assessment B – Evaluation Scorecard

## Scoring scale

The exercises used a 1–5 scale for each criterion. The following is a practical interpretation for this learning portfolio; it is not claimed to be a universal scoring standard.

| Score | General interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Very poor – major problem |
| 2 | Poor – significant weaknesses |
| 3 | Fair – partly meets expectations, but has notable gaps |
| 4 | Good – meets expectations with minor issues |
| 5 | Excellent – fully meets expectations |

## How to interpret the scorecard

- **E01:** Incorrect threshold and checkout guidance made the response unacceptable.
- **E02:** The core fact was correct, but the answer did not fully explain the required next step.
- **E03:** The core answer was correct, but extra processing-time and email claims were unsupported.
- **E04:** The answer was correct and useful, but did not explicitly address the selection restriction.
- **E05:** The core answer was correct, but the extra verification statement was unsupported and potentially misleading.

## Scoring reminders

1. Score the whole response, not only its first sentence.
2. Do not automatically give every criterion the same score.
3. A clear response can still be inaccurate.
4. A relevant response can still be wrong.
5. An accurate response can still be incomplete.
6. Explain each score with evidence from the requirements and response.

The overall verdict is a separate judgement. Do not calculate it by blindly averaging the four dimension scores. Consider the severity and impact of critical errors.
